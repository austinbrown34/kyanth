How the three bars move
The peak bar reads now
The blue centre bar is the live input level, growing from the middle axis in both directions. It is the only bar with a glow, and the glow tracks amplitude — a microphone hearing nothing sits flat and dark.
The silver bars are the same signal, delayed
Left replays the level from 70 ms ago, right from 130 ms ago. Nothing is random: a syllable enters at the centre and travels outward, so the mark ripples in time with your voice instead of jittering.
At rest it is exactly the icon
Between dictations the bars settle back to the icon's own proportions. The overlay never becomes a different object — it is the app icon breathing.

What changed from the first pass

  • The dot and the generic meter are gone. A coloured status dot next to a row of bars was two symbols doing one job. Now there is one symbol — the app's own mark — and its shape carries the state.
  • The plate is the icon's ground. Same slate gradient, same top sheen, so the thing that appears under the notch is recognisably this app before you read a word of it.
  • Motion has a rule, not a random walk. Centre reads live, the flanks replay it 70 and 130 ms late. That is what makes it read as a voice passing through rather than three bars twitching.
  • Transcribing is the menu-bar glyph, enlarged. The bars collapse to three stubs and cycle — the same shape the status item shows for the same state, so the two surfaces teach each other.

Rules it still obeys

  • It never becomes key window. Functional, not cosmetic: focus theft would redirect the dictation into the overlay. Notes keeps its active title bar the whole time.
  • It carries text. "Model server not responding" and "Copied — press ⌘V" are outcomes the sound cues alone cannot express.
  • It is the second channel for a hidden status item — all six states resolve here, so a full menu bar costs the user nothing.
  • Docked under the notch on notched displays, top-centre otherwise. Springs in, fades out; state changes stop snapping.
