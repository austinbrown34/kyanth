Why a table beat the list-and-detail split

  • Everything is comparable at a glance. Time, what you said, how long you spoke, where it landed and how long transcription took are columns, not one undifferentiated string. The old two-pane version could only ever show you one entry's facts at a time.
  • Select expands in place. No detail pane sitting empty on the right — the row opens under itself with the whole transcription, its facts, and Copy / Paste again / Delete. Click it again to collapse.
  • The Spoken column is the meter, applied to the archive. A bar as wide as the time you spent talking, so an eleven-second paragraph is findable without reading a word. It stays neutral until the row is selected, then it lights with the peak.
  • Filter by outcome. Pasted / clipboard / nothing-heard are already in the app's state model — Clipboard is the list you open when text went missing.
  • Search, day headings, per-row delete, an empty state. All four were absent; nothing truncates at 96 characters any more.

Try it: click a row to open it, type in Search, or switch to Nothing heard.
